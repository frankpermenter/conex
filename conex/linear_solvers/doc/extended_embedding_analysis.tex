\section{Setup}

Consider the extended embedding of a conic program with cone $\mathcal{C} = \R^n_+$:
\begin{align}
  \min \quad & \alpha \theta \notag \\
  \text{s.t.} \quad & Ax - b\tau = r_p \theta \label{eq:primal} \\
  & -A^T y - s + c\tau = r_d \theta \label{eq:dual} \\
  & \ip{b}{y} - \ip{c}{x} - \kappa = r_g \theta \label{eq:gap} \\
  & \ip{r_p}{y} + \ip{r_d}{x} + r_g \tau = -\alpha \label{eq:norm} \\
  & x \in \R^n_+, \; s \in \R^n_+, \; \tau \geq 0, \; \kappa \geq 0 \notag
\end{align}
where $A \in \R^{m \times n}$, $b \in \R^m$, $c \in \R^n$, and $y \in \R^m$, $\theta \in \R$ are free variables.

\section{Self-duality}

The embedding is self-dual: if $(x, s, y, \tau, \kappa, \theta)$ is feasible, then swapping
\[
  (x, s, \tau, \kappa, y) \;\longleftrightarrow\; (s, x, \kappa, \tau, -y)
\]
gives a dual-feasible point. This symmetry is the key structural property.

\section{Geodesic parametrization}

For the product cone $\R^n_+ \times \R^n_+ \times \R_+ \times \R_+$ with rank $R = 2n + 2$, the geodesic parametrization at scaling $W = \diag(w_x, w_s, w_\tau, w_\kappa)$ and barrier parameter $k = 1/\sqrt{\mu}$ gives:
\begin{align}
  \text{slack}_i &= \frac{1 - d_i}{k \, W_i}, \label{eq:slack} \\
  \lambda_i &= k \, W_i \, (1 + d_i). \label{eq:dual_var}
\end{align}

For the $x \geq 0$ constraint, the ``slack'' is $x$ itself and $\lambda_x$ is its dual. For $s \geq 0$, the slack is $s$ and $\lambda_s$ is its dual.

\section{Self-duality implies $d_x = -d_s$}

By self-duality, $x$ is simultaneously:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The slack of the $x \geq 0$ constraint: $x_i = \frac{1 - d_{x,i}}{k \, w_{x,i}}$.
  \item The dual variable of the $s \geq 0$ constraint: $x_i = k \, w_{s,i} \, (1 + d_{s,i})$.
\end{enumerate}
Symmetrically, $s$ is:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The slack of $s \geq 0$: $s_i = \frac{1 - d_{s,i}}{k \, w_{s,i}}$.
  \item The dual of $x \geq 0$: $s_i = k \, w_{x,i} \, (1 + d_{x,i})$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Nesterov--Todd scaling}

Impose the NT scaling $w_{x,i} = w_{s,i}^{-1}$. Then from identifications (1) and (2) for $x$:
\[
  \frac{1 - d_{x,i}}{k \, w_{x,i}} = k \, w_{s,i} \, (1 + d_{s,i}) = k \, w_{x,i}^{-1} \, (1 + d_{s,i}).
\]
Multiply both sides by $k \, w_{x,i}$:
\[
  1 - d_{x,i} = k^2 \, (1 + d_{s,i}).
\]
Similarly from $s$:
\[
  \frac{1 - d_{s,i}}{k \, w_{s,i}} = k \, w_{x,i} \, (1 + d_{x,i}).
\]
Multiply by $k \, w_{s,i} = k \, w_{x,i}^{-1}$:
\[
  1 - d_{s,i} = k^2 \, (1 + d_{x,i}).
\]
Adding both equations:
\[
  (1 - d_{x,i}) + (1 - d_{s,i}) = k^2 \bigl[(1 + d_{s,i}) + (1 + d_{x,i})\bigr].
\]
\[
  2 - (d_{x,i} + d_{s,i}) = k^2 \bigl[2 + (d_{x,i} + d_{s,i})\bigr].
\]
Let $\sigma = d_{x,i} + d_{s,i}$:
\[
  2 - \sigma = k^2(2 + \sigma) \implies \sigma(1 + k^2) = 2(1 - k^2) \implies \sigma = \frac{2(1-k^2)}{1+k^2}.
\]
From the first equation alone:
\[
  1 - d_{x,i} = k^2(1 + d_{s,i}) \implies d_{x,i} = 1 - k^2 - k^2 d_{s,i}.
\]
From the second:
\[
  d_{s,i} = 1 - k^2 - k^2 d_{x,i}.
\]
Substituting:
\[
  d_{s,i} = 1 - k^2 - k^2(1 - k^2 - k^2 d_{s,i}) = (1-k^2)(1 - k^2) + k^4 d_{s,i}.
\]
\[
  d_{s,i}(1 - k^4) = (1-k^2)^2 \implies d_{s,i} = \frac{(1-k^2)^2}{(1-k^2)(1+k^2)} = \frac{1 - k^2}{1 + k^2}.
\]
This gives $d_{s,i}$ as a \emph{constant} (independent of $i$), which is too strong. The issue is that identifying $x$ simultaneously as slack of $x \geq 0$ and dual of $s \geq 0$ over-constrains the system at general $k$.

\subsection{Correct identification at $k = 1$}

At $k = 1$ (the initial barrier), the equations give $\sigma = 0$, so $d_{x,i} = -d_{s,i}$. More generally, the self-dual structure means the central path satisfies $x \circ s = \mu e$ and $\tau \kappa = \mu$, which in the geodesic parametrization becomes $d_x = -d_s$ and $d_\tau = -d_\kappa$ \textbf{at every point on the central path}.

To see this directly: on the central path,
\[
  x_i s_i = \mu, \quad \text{i.e.,} \quad \frac{(1 - d_{x,i})(1 - d_{s,i})}{k^2 w_{x,i} w_{s,i}} \cdot k^2 w_{x,i} w_{s,i} = \frac{(1-d_{x,i})(1+d_{x,i})}{1} = \mu
\]
Wait --- this is $x_i$ as slack times $\lambda_{x,i}$ as dual of the same constraint:
\[
  x_i \cdot \lambda_{x,i} = \frac{1 - d_{x,i}}{k w_{x,i}} \cdot k w_{x,i}(1 + d_{x,i}) = (1 - d_{x,i})(1+d_{x,i}) = 1 - d_{x,i}^2 = \mu.
\]
This gives $d_{x,i}^2 = 1 - \mu$ for each $i$ on the central path, but doesn't directly relate $d_x$ to $d_s$.

\subsection{Direct argument from the Newton equations}

The clearest argument: the Newton direction at $(W, k)$ solves a linear system. By the self-duality of the embedding, the solution inherits the symmetry. Specifically, if we apply the primal-dual swap $(x, s, \tau, \kappa) \to (s, x, \kappa, \tau)$ and $y \to -y$ to the Newton equations, and if $W$ satisfies $w_x = w_s^{-1}$ and $w_\tau = w_\kappa^{-1}$ (NT scaling for both pairs), then the transformed direction $(\tilde{d}_x, \tilde{d}_s) = (-d_s, -d_x)$ also solves the Newton equations. By uniqueness:
\[
  \boxed{d_x = -d_s, \qquad d_\tau = -d_\kappa.}
\]

\section{Implications for the KKT system}

\subsection{Reduced dimension}

With $d_s = -d_x$ and $d_\kappa = -d_\tau$, the direction has $n + 1$ independent cone components instead of $2n + 2$. The effective rank is
\[
  R_{\text{eff}} = n + 1.
\]

\subsection{Elimination of $s$ and $\kappa$}

Since $d_{s,i} = -d_{x,i}$, from \eqref{eq:slack}:
\[
  s_i = \frac{1 - d_{s,i}}{k w_{s,i}} = \frac{1 + d_{x,i}}{k w_{x,i}^{-1}} = k w_{x,i}(1 + d_{x,i}) = \lambda_{x,i}.
\]
So \textbf{$s = \lambda_x$}: the dual slack equals the dual variable of $x \geq 0$. Similarly $\kappa = \lambda_\tau$.

Substituting into the dual equality \eqref{eq:dual}:
\[
  -A^T y - \lambda_x + c\tau = r_d \theta.
\]
This is the standard dual feasibility condition with $s$ eliminated.

\subsection{Elimination of $d$}

The direction $d_x$ is not an independent variable --- it is determined by the primal variables through \eqref{eq:dx}:
\[
  d_{x,i} = 1 - w_{x,i} \cdot x_i.
\]
Substituting into the expressions for the dual:
\[
  \lambda_{x,i} = k \, w_{x,i}(1 + d_{x,i}) = k \, w_{x,i}(2 - w_{x,i} x_i) = 2k \, w_{x,i} - k \, w_{x,i}^2 \, x_i.
\]
Since $s_i = \lambda_{x,i}$:
\begin{equation} \label{eq:s_from_x}
  s_i = 2k \, w_{x,i} - k \, w_{x,i}^2 \, x_i.
\end{equation}
Similarly:
\begin{equation} \label{eq:kappa_from_tau}
  \kappa = 2k \, w_\tau - k \, w_\tau^2 \, \tau.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Reduced system in $(x, y, \tau, \theta)$}

Substituting \eqref{eq:s_from_x} and \eqref{eq:kappa_from_tau} into the equality constraints \eqref{eq:primal}--\eqref{eq:norm}, we eliminate $s$, $\kappa$, and $d$ entirely. The independent variables are
\[
  (x, y, \tau, \theta) \in \R^{n + m + 2}
\]
with cone constraints $x \geq 0$, $\tau \geq 0$ (rank $n + 1$).

\paragraph{Equality \eqref{eq:primal}} (unchanged):
\begin{equation}
  Ax - b\tau = r_p \theta. \tag{E1}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Equality \eqref{eq:dual}} with $s$ from \eqref{eq:s_from_x}:
\[
  -A^T y - (2k w_x - k W_x^2 x) + c\tau = r_d \theta
\]
\begin{equation}
  k W_x^2 x - A^T y + c\tau = r_d \theta + 2k w_x. \tag{E2}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Equality \eqref{eq:gap}} with $\kappa$ from \eqref{eq:kappa_from_tau}:
\[
  \ip{b}{y} - \ip{c}{x} - (2k w_\tau - k w_\tau^2 \tau) = r_g \theta
\]
\begin{equation}
  -\ip{c}{x} + \ip{b}{y} + k w_\tau^2 \tau = r_g \theta + 2k w_\tau. \tag{E3}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Equality \eqref{eq:norm}} (unchanged --- involves only $x, y, \tau$):
\begin{equation}
  \ip{r_d}{x} + \ip{r_p}{y} + r_g \tau = -\alpha. \tag{E4}
\end{equation}

\subsection{KKT system}

Note that (E2) already contains the term $k W_x^2 x$, which is $k$ times the $x$-block of the Gram matrix applied to $x$. Similarly (E3) contains $k w_\tau^2 \tau$. This means the equality constraints absorb the Gram contributions at barrier parameter $k$.

The constraint Jacobian $J$ (rows = constraints, columns = variables $(x, y, \tau, \theta)$) is:
\[
  J = \begin{pmatrix}
    A & 0 & -b & -r_p \\
    kW_x^2 & -A^T & c & r_d \\
    -c^T & b^T & kw_\tau^2 & -r_g \\
    r_d^T & r_p^T & r_g & 0
  \end{pmatrix}.
\]
The Gram matrix from the cone constraints $x \geq 0$, $\tau \geq 0$ is
\[
  G = \diag(W_x^2,\; 0_m,\; w_\tau^2,\; 0).
\]
The full KKT system $\begin{psmallmatrix} G & J^T \\ J & 0 \end{psmallmatrix} \begin{psmallmatrix} z \\ \nu \end{psmallmatrix} = \begin{psmallmatrix} g \\ h \end{psmallmatrix}$ is:
\begin{equation} \label{eq:reduced_kkt}
  \begin{pmatrix}
    W_x^2 & & & & A^T & k W_x^2 & -c & r_d \\
    & 0 & & & 0 & -A & b & r_p \\
    & & w_\tau^2 & & -b^T & c^T & k w_\tau^2 & r_g \\
    & & & 0 & -r_p^T & r_d^T & -r_g & 0 \\
    A & 0 & -b & -r_p & & & & \\
    kW_x^2 & -A^T & c & r_d & & & & \\
    -c^T & b^T & kw_\tau^2 & -r_g & & & & \\
    r_d^T & r_p^T & r_g & 0 & & & &
  \end{pmatrix}
  \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \\ \tau \\ \theta \\ \nu_p \\ \nu_d \\ \nu_g \\ \nu_n \end{pmatrix}
  =
  \begin{pmatrix}
    2w_x \\ 0 \\ 2w_\tau \\ -k\alpha \\
    0 \\ 2kw_x \\ 2kw_\tau \\ -\alpha
  \end{pmatrix}
\end{equation}
where $W_x = \diag(w_x)$, $\nu = (\nu_p, \nu_d, \nu_g, \nu_n)$ are the duals of constraints (E1)--(E4), and the RHS incorporates the $2kw_x$ and $2kw_\tau$ terms from the substitution of $s$ and $\kappa$.

Note: the top-right block is $J^T$ and the bottom-left is $J$, as required by KKT symmetry.

\section{Elimination of $x$}

The cone variable $x$ has invertible Gram block $W_x^2$. From the stationarity condition (row~1 of \eqref{eq:reduced_kkt}):
\begin{equation} \label{eq:x_stationarity}
  W_x^2 x + A^T \nu_p + k W_x^2 \nu_d - c \nu_g + r_d \nu_n = 2 w_x.
\end{equation}
Solving for $x$ with $D = W_x^{-2}$:
\begin{equation} \label{eq:x_elim}
  x = 2 W_x^{-1} - D A^T \nu_p - k \nu_d + D c \, \nu_g - D r_d \, \nu_n.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Substitution into constraints}

Substitute \eqref{eq:x_elim} into each constraint row.

\paragraph{(E1): $Ax - b\tau - r_p \theta = 0$.}
\begin{align}
  &A\bigl(2W_x^{-1} - DA^T \nu_p - k\nu_d + Dc\,\nu_g - Dr_d\,\nu_n\bigr) - b\tau - r_p\theta = 0 \notag\\
  &\Rightarrow\quad -ADA^T\,\nu_p - kA\,\nu_d + ADc\,\nu_g - ADr_d\,\nu_n - b\tau - r_p\theta = -2AW_x^{-1}. \label{eq:e1_elim}
\end{align}

\paragraph{(E2): $kW_x^2 x - A^Ty + c\tau + r_d\theta = 2kw_x$.}
Since $kW_x^2 \cdot D = kI$ and $kW_x^2 \cdot W_x^{-1} = kw_x$:
\begin{align}
  &2kw_x - kA^T\nu_p - k^2W_x^2\nu_d + kc\,\nu_g - kr_d\,\nu_n - A^Ty + c\tau + r_d\theta = 2kw_x \notag\\
  &\Rightarrow\quad -kA^T\nu_p - k^2W_x^2\nu_d + kc\,\nu_g - kr_d\,\nu_n - A^Ty + c\tau + r_d\theta = 0. \label{eq:e2_elim}
\end{align}
The constant terms cancel.

\paragraph{(E3): $-c^Tx + b^Ty + kw_\tau^2\tau - r_g\theta = 2kw_\tau$.}
\begin{align}
  &-c^T\bigl(2W_x^{-1} - DA^T\nu_p - k\nu_d + Dc\,\nu_g - Dr_d\,\nu_n\bigr) + b^Ty + kw_\tau^2\tau - r_g\theta = 2kw_\tau \notag\\
  &\Rightarrow\quad c^TDA^T\nu_p + kc^T\nu_d - c^TDc\,\nu_g + c^TDr_d\,\nu_n + b^Ty + kw_\tau^2\tau - r_g\theta = 2kw_\tau + 2c^TW_x^{-1}. \label{eq:e3_elim}
\end{align}

\paragraph{(E4): $r_d^Tx + r_p^Ty + r_g\tau = -\alpha$.}
\begin{align}
  &\Rightarrow\quad -r_d^TDA^T\nu_p - kr_d^T\nu_d + r_d^TDc\,\nu_g - r_d^TDr_d\,\nu_n + r_p^Ty + r_g\tau = -\alpha - 2r_d^TW_x^{-1}. \label{eq:e4_elim}
\end{align}

\subsection{Reduced system}

The remaining stationarity conditions (rows 2--4 of \eqref{eq:reduced_kkt}) do not involve $x$:
\begin{align}
  \text{row 2 (y):} \quad & -A\,\nu_d + b\,\nu_g + r_p\,\nu_n = 0, \label{eq:y_stat}\\
  \text{row 3 ($\tau$):} \quad & w_\tau^2\tau - b^T\nu_p + c^T\nu_d + kw_\tau^2\nu_g + r_g\nu_n = 2w_\tau, \label{eq:tau_stat}\\
  \text{row 4 ($\theta$):} \quad & -r_p^T\nu_p + r_d^T\nu_d - r_g\nu_g = -k\alpha. \label{eq:theta_stat}
\end{align}

Equations \eqref{eq:e1_elim}--\eqref{eq:e4_elim} and \eqref{eq:y_stat}--\eqref{eq:theta_stat} form a system in $(y, \tau, \theta, \nu_p, \nu_d, \nu_g, \nu_n)$ of size $2m + n + 3$.

Define the \emph{Schur complement matrix} $S = ADA^T = AW_x^{-2}A^T \in \R^{m \times m}$ (symmetric positive semidefinite). The constraint block after elimination is:
\begin{equation} \label{eq:elim_system}
\begin{pmatrix}
  0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & -A & b & r_p \\
  0 & w_\tau^2 & 0 & -b^T & c^T & kw_\tau^2 & r_g \\
  0 & 0 & 0 & -r_p^T & r_d^T & -r_g & 0 \\
  0 & -b & -r_p & -S & -kA & ADc & -ADr_d \\
  -A^T & c & r_d & -kA^T & -k^2W_x^2 & kc & -kr_d \\
  b^T & kw_\tau^2 & -r_g & c^TDA^T & kc^T & -c^TDc & c^TDr_d \\
  r_p^T & r_g & 0 & -r_d^TDA^T & -kr_d^T & r_d^TDc & -r_d^TDr_d
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix} y \\ \tau \\ \theta \\ \nu_p \\ \nu_d \\ \nu_g \\ \nu_n \end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 2w_\tau \\ -k\alpha \\ -2AW_x^{-1} \\ 0 \\ 2kw_\tau + 2c^TW_x^{-1} \\ -\alpha - 2r_d^TW_x^{-1} \end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Key observation.} The expensive block is $S = AW_x^{-2}A^T$ ($m \times m$), which is exactly the normal-equations matrix from standard LP interior-point methods. When $m \ll n$, this is a significant reduction: we have eliminated $n$ variables ($x$) at the cost of a dense $m \times m$ Schur complement.

\subsection{Dimensions}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
  & Full embedding & Reduced (NT) & $x$-eliminated \\
  \hline
  Primal variables & $2n + m + 3$ & $n + m + 2$ & $m + 2$ \\
  Equality duals & $m + n + 2$ & $m + n + 2$ & $m + n + 2$ \\
  Total system size & $3n + 2m + 5$ & $2n + 2m + 4$ & $2m + n + 3$ \\
  Cone rank (RowSpace) & $2n + 2$ & $n + 1$ & $1$ ($\tau$ only) \\
  Dense block & --- & --- & $S = AW_x^{-2}A^T$ ($m \times m$) \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

For the original LP with $m$ constraints and $n$ variables, the $x$-eliminated system has size $2m + n + 3$, which is smaller than the NT-reduced system ($2n + 2m + 4$) whenever $n > 1$. The dominant cost is forming and factoring the Schur complement $S \in \R^{m \times m}$.

\subsection{Recovery}

Given the solution $(y^*, \tau^*, \theta^*, \nu^*)$ of \eqref{eq:elim_system}, recover $x$ from \eqref{eq:x_elim}:
\[
  x^* = 2W_x^{-1} - DA^T\nu_p^* - k\nu_d^* + Dc\,\nu_g^* - Dr_d\,\nu_n^*.
\]
Then from $(x^*, y^*, \tau^*, \theta^*)$, recover:
\begin{align}
  d_{x,i} &= 1 - w_{x,i} x^*_i, \\
  d_\tau &= 1 - w_\tau \tau^*, \\
  s^*_i &= 2k w_{x,i} - k w_{x,i}^2 x^*_i = k w_{x,i}(2 - w_{x,i} x^*_i) = k w_{x,i}(1 + d_{x,i}), \\
  \kappa^* &= k w_\tau(1 + d_\tau), \\
  d_{s,i} &= -d_{x,i}, \quad d_\kappa = -d_\tau.
\end{align}
The complementarity gap is
\[
  \mu \bigl((n+1) - \|d_x\|^2 - d_\tau^2\bigr) = \frac{1}{k^2}\bigl((n+1) - \|d_x\|^2 - d_\tau^2\bigr).
\]

\section{Structure of the $\theta$ update}

\subsection{$\theta$ as duality gap residual}

From the gap constraint (E3) before eliminating $s$ and $\kappa$:
\begin{equation} \label{eq:theta_gap}
  \theta = \frac{\ip{b}{y} - \ip{c}{x} - \kappa}{r_g}.
\end{equation}
So $\theta$ measures the \emph{residual duality gap}: the quantity $\ip{b}{y} - \ip{c}{x} - \kappa$ is exactly $r_g \theta$. At the fixed point ($\theta = 1$), this recovers the definition of $r_g$. At optimality ($\theta = 0$), we get $\ip{b}{y} = \ip{c}{x} + \kappa$, and if $\tau > 0$ with $\kappa \to 0$, this yields strong duality for the original LP.

Similarly, the primal and dual residuals are proportional to $\theta$:
\begin{align}
  Ax - b\tau &= r_p \theta \quad \text{(primal residual)}, \\
  -A^Ty - s + c\tau &= r_d \theta \quad \text{(dual residual)}.
\end{align}
All three residuals vanish simultaneously as $\theta \to 0$.

\subsection{$\theta$-free equations}

In the $x$-eliminated system \eqref{eq:elim_system}, $\theta$ appears in rows 4--6 (the eliminated E1--E3) but \textbf{not} in:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Row 1: $y$-stationarity \eqref{eq:y_stat},
  \item Row 2: $\tau$-stationarity \eqref{eq:tau_stat},
  \item Row 3: $\theta$-stationarity \eqref{eq:theta_stat},
  \item Row 7: the normalization constraint (E4) \eqref{eq:e4_elim}.
\end{itemize}
These four equations constrain $(y, \tau, \nu)$ independently of $\theta$. Once $(y, \tau, \nu)$ are determined, $\theta$ is recovered from any of E1--E3; the scalar E3 is most convenient:
\begin{equation} \label{eq:theta_recover}
  \theta = \frac{1}{r_g}\Bigl(c^TDA^T\nu_p + kc^T\nu_d - c^TDc\,\nu_g + c^TDr_d\,\nu_n + b^Ty + kw_\tau^2\tau - 2kw_\tau - 2c^TW_x^{-1}\Bigr).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Predictor step from a centered point}

At a centered point ($d_x = 0$, $d_\tau = 0$), the Newton step produces a direction $(\delta d_x, \delta d_\tau, \delta \theta)$. The geodesic parametrization requires $|d_{x,i} + \delta d_{x,i}| < 1$ and $|d_\tau + \delta d_\tau| < 1$, so from centering: $|\delta d_{x,i}| < 1$, $|\delta d_\tau| < 1$.

The cone rank is $R = n + 1$ (from $x \in \R^n_+$ and $\tau \geq 0$). By the standard self-dual IPM analysis, the predictor direction satisfies
\[
  \|\delta d\|^2 = \sum_i (\delta d_{x,i})^2 + (\delta d_\tau)^2 \;\propto\; (\delta \theta / \theta)^2 \cdot R.
\]
A safe step (keeping $\|d\|_\infty < 1$) yields
\[
  |\delta \theta| \leq \frac{\theta}{\sqrt{R}} = \frac{\theta}{\sqrt{n+1}},
\]
giving the update
\begin{equation} \label{eq:theta_update}
  \boxed{\theta_{\mathrm{new}} \approx \theta \left(1 - \frac{1}{\sqrt{n+1}}\right).}
\end{equation}
This is the standard $O(\sqrt{n} \log(1/\varepsilon))$ convergence rate for self-dual embeddings, with the effective cone rank $n + 1$ (not the original $2n + 2$) thanks to the NT reduction $d_x = -d_s$.
